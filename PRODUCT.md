# Product

## Register

product — the primary surface is the dashboard app (feed, pipeline, analytics, settings). The landing page and auth screens are the one brand surface; treat them in the brand register per task.

## Users & Purpose

UK and remote freelancers (developers, designers, writers, marketers) actively hunting contract work. Flaiir scrapes 20+ job sources on a schedule, scores every lead against the user's skills and rate, and gives them a feed → pipeline → analytics loop: find the right leads fast, work them like deals, and see whether the effort pays.

Context of use: short, frequent sessions — a freelancer checking between client work, often triaging dozens of leads in minutes. Speed of scanning and acting beats richness of display. The one number they ultimately care about: money won through the pipeline.

## Brand personality

Sharp, honest, working-class-tool. Flaiir talks like a fellow freelancer, not a SaaS marketing deck. Numbers are honest (caveats at low sample sizes, no fake precision, "~" on estimates). One accent, used sparingly.

## Anti-references

- Icon-heavy dashboards (owner has explicitly rejected "too many icons")
- Marketing-poster analytics — must read like a professional CRM (Pipedrive/Attio), not a landing page
- AI-slop SaaS defaults: purple gradients, glassmorphism, hero-metric templates, eyebrow labels on every section
- Overclaiming copy ("25+ sources", promises of emails that don't exist) — every claim in the UI must be true

## Strategic design principles

1. **Triage-first density** — the user is scanning volume; compact, scannable, keyboard-friendly beats spacious.
2. **One accent (lime), ≤10% of any screen** — status colors (green win, coral loss, amber mid) are reserved for meaning.
3. **Numbers are mono, tabular, and honest** — deltas vs previous period, caveats when samples are thin.
4. **Every metric must be able to change what the user does next** — descriptive-only charts get cut.
5. **Self-serve trust** — errors are distinguished from empty states; the UI never blames the user or lies about state.
